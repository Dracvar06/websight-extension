Setting up WebSight

For the sighted friend helping with the setup. It takes about 30 to 45 minutes, most of it waiting for downloads. Once it is done, the blind person uses WebSight entirely by voice and keyboard.

What WebSight is: a Chrome extension. Press Option + Shift + S (Alt + Shift + S on Windows), speak, and an AI looks at the page and answers out loud: what the page is, where things are, what a date or price is. It can also click, open menus, search a site and take you to websites. It always puts the screen reader on the line its answer came from, so the person can check it.

What you need

  • A Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or newer). 16 GB of memory or more if you want the free, private AI that runs on the computer itself.
  • Google Chrome.
  • The WebSight folder on the computer.
  • About 15 GB of free disk space for the local helpers (optional, but recommended).

Step 1. Add WebSight to Chrome

  1. In Chrome, open chrome://extensions (type it in the address bar).
  2. Turn on Developer mode, top right.
  3. Click Load unpacked and choose the WebSight folder.
  4. Click the puzzle-piece icon to the right of the address bar and pin WebSight, so its icon is always there. WebSight opens in Chrome's side panel, on the right of the window, and stays open while the page is used.
  5. Open chrome://extensions/shortcuts and check that WebSight's "Talk" shortcut shows Option + Shift + S. If the box is empty, click it and press those keys. (Chrome leaves it empty when another extension already uses them.)

After any update to the WebSight folder, come back to chrome://extensions and press WebSight's reload arrow.

Step 2. Install the free helpers on the computer

These run only on this computer and cost nothing:

  • Voice server: understands speech in any language and speaks with natural voices. Without it, WebSight uses Chrome's speech recognition (which sends the voice to Google) and the Mac's built-in voices.
  • Search engine: lets "take me to the admissions page" find the right page directly. Without it, WebSight uses the site's own list of pages, which works on fewer sites.
  • Local AI (Ollama): a free, private AI that answers when the cloud AI is busy, or all the time if you prefer.
  1. Install Homebrew if the Mac does not have it: follow the one-line instruction at brew.sh.
  2. For the local AI, install the Ollama app from ollama.com and open it once.
  3. Open the Terminal app, go to the WebSight folder and run the setup script. If the folder is in Projects, for example: 
    cd ~/Projects/websight-extension
./setup-local-servers.sh
    It downloads about 10 GB the first time (the speech model and the local AI are the large parts), so give it time. It can be run again safely: anything already installed is skipped. When it finishes, both servers start by themselves every time the Mac starts.

Step 3. Choose the AI

Right-click the WebSight icon and choose Options. Section 1 of the settings page, "The AI that reads pages for you", is where this goes. Choose one:

Option A (recommended): Gemini, paid by a Google AI Pro plan

Fast, sees the screen, and reliable. Google's free tier is often too busy, so this uses the paid tier, which a Google AI Pro subscription's monthly Cloud credit can cover.

  1. Create a key at Google AI Studio (Create API key). Copy it.
  2. In WebSight's settings, choose Gemini, paste the key, and press Test connection.
  3. To move the key off the free tier: open the Google Developer Program profile with the same Google account that has the AI Pro plan, create a profile if asked, then open Benefits and activate the monthly Google Cloud credit. It asks for a Cloud billing account; create one if needed.
  4. In the Google Cloud billing page, set a budget alert (for example 10 dollars a month), so any cost beyond the credit is noticed at once.
  5. Back in AI Studio, on the API keys page, click Set up billing next to the key and choose that billing account. The key's tier should change from "Free tier" to a paid tier.
Do not paste the key anywhere except WebSight's settings. It stays on this computer. On the free tier, Google may use what WebSight sends (the text of the pages) to improve its products; on the paid tier it does not.

Option B: completely free and private

Choose Ollama. Nothing leaves the computer. Answers take 5 to 20 seconds instead of 2 to 5, and it is less good at finding its way around complicated sites.

Option C: another service

Choose Groq, OpenRouter, OpenAI or any other service (some have free tiers with limits), or Claude (very reliable, paid per use). Paste the key, press Test connection, and type one of the models it lists, choosing one that can see images. "Any other AI service" accepts almost any provider: paste its address, which usually ends in /v1.

Step 4. The rest of the settings

  1. About you (section 2): write a few lines about the person, for example "I am a blind student at UCSB. I use VoiceOver. I often use Técnico Lisboa's website. I speak English, Catalan and Spanish." It helps WebSight guess right and hear names right.
  2. Listening (section 3): press Enable microphone and allow it. If the person always speaks one language, choose it.
  3. Voice (section 4): set the speaking speed (screen reader users often like 2x or more) and press Test voice.
  4. Seeing the screen (section 5): for someone with some sight, choose a larger text size and the colours that are easiest to read (high contrast is yellow and white on black), and consider turning on the large-text strip. For a screen reader user who prefers their own screen reader's voice and speed, choose it under "Who reads the answers aloud".
  5. Finding pages (section 6): leave SearXNG chosen and press Test search. It should name a UCSB admissions page.

Step 5. Try it together

Open any website, press Option + Shift + S, wait for the beep, speak, then press Enter. Pauses are fine; it waits for Enter (or the same keys again). The panel stays open on the right; clicking the page does not close it. Try:

  • "What is this page?"
  • "When is the application deadline?" (it answers and puts the screen reader on that line)
  • "Take me to the admissions page."
  • On a new tab: "Take me to the UChicago website." (it only goes to official sites, and asks first if unsure)
  • "Search this site for parking."
  • "Remember that I am a physics major."
  • "Click Buy now." (anything that buys, sends, deletes or submits is read back first, and only happens after a "yes")

Shortcuts

  • From any page: Option + Shift + S talk (again to send), N new conversation, R repeat, . (period) stop speaking, all with Option + Shift.
  • In the panel: Escape stops speaking; Option + Shift with the up or down arrow speaks faster or slower, with + or - makes the text bigger or smaller, and with H lists every shortcut aloud.
  • By voice: "repeat that", "slower", "faster", "stop".
  • Faster and slower can also get shortcuts that work from the page: set them at chrome://extensions/shortcuts.

If something goes wrong

  • The shortcut does nothing: see the last point of Step 1.
  • "Busy" or "at its limit": the cloud AI is overloaded; the local AI answers instead if Ollama is running. If it happens a lot with Gemini, the key is probably still on the free tier (Step 3, Option A).
  • It mishears: choose the language in section 3, and add names it gets wrong to About you.
  • "Chrome does not let extensions read" a page: Chrome's own pages (new tab, settings, the Web Store) are closed to all extensions. Say a website to go to instead.
  • No voice, or a robotic voice: the voice server is not running. Run the setup script from Step 2 again.
  • Check everything at once: on the settings page, press Test connection, Test search and Test voice.
